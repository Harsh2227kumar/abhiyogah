# Building the RBI complaint engine nobody ships

**As of October 2026, RBI's complaint rules have become a set of interlocking, date-arithmetic obligations. They include a 30-day reply clock, a 20-day automatic escalation to the Internal Ombudsman (IO), a 90-day filing window, 15- and 30-day Ombudsman-stage clocks, ₹100 a day for late failed-transaction reversals, a 10-working-day shadow credit and a 90-day fraud-liability decision. No commercial product, open-source repository or published reference architecture found in this research executes them as dated, clause-cited rules on individual complaints.** The binding texts are the Reserve Bank Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RB-IOS) 2026 (in force 1 July 2026), the Commercial Banks Internal Ombudsman Directions 2026 (14 January 2026), and the Responsible Business Conduct Directions 2025 (RBC-CB), updated to 1 October 2026. Around them sit the 2019 failed-transaction turnaround-time (TAT) circular and adjacent fraud, KYC, cyber, data-protection and AI regimes. A change to fraud liability is already notified for 1 January 2027. Enforcement concentrates on exactly these deterministic clocks. SBI, Central Bank of India and Union Bank of India were each fined between ₹95 lakh and ₹1.73 crore partly for missing the 10-day shadow credit or the 90-day compensation deadline. RBI's Governor and a Deputy Governor have publicly named the remaining failures: complaints misclassified as queries, IO bypass, and closure treated as resolution. The market splits in two. Case-management CRMs run generic TAT matrices. Regulatory-intelligence tools map rule text to controls and never reach the case. Every RBI-specific GitHub project reviewed is a 0–10-star prototype. CustomerPulse's engine has the right skeleton: a rules file, stored evidence, explanation endpoints and a regulatory document pipeline. Code review, however, shows it currently produces unreliable compliance signals. It treats AI enrichment as both acknowledgement and resolution, runs on a US CFPB-shaped data model, evaluates once, has 9 of 17 rules that cannot fire, and flags its own legally required reply wording as a legal-risk event. This report sets out a 59-capability specification for a complete RBI Compliance Engine. Against it, CustomerPulse has 2 capabilities Built, 15 Partial and 42 Missing, and for 25 of the 59 no counterpart was found in any reviewed product or repository.

## RBI's complaint rulebook now runs on five interlocking clocks

The regulatory centre of gravity moved three times between November 2025 and July 2026. On 28 November 2025, RBI consolidated about 9,000 circulars into roughly 244 entity-wise Master Directions on an "as-is" basis and repealed about 9,445 instruments ([SNG & Partners](https://sngpartners.in/2025/12/04/rbi-rolls-out-consolidated-master-directions/); [TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/49882/rbi-notified-244-new-master-directions-and-repealed-withdrew-9445-circulars/)). Customer-service, limited-liability, deceased-claim, property-document and recovery rules for commercial banks now sit in RBC-CB (RBI/DOR/2025-26/170) ([RBC-CB text](https://www.caalley.com/rbi_mc_md_25/md/170MD.pdf)). On 14 January 2026, RBI issued harmonised Internal Ombudsman Directions per entity type, replacing the 29 December 2023 Master Direction ([IO Directions, SBI-hosted copy](https://sbi.bank.in/documents/53471/53742/11082026_Annexure+I-RBI+Commercial+Banks-Internal+Ombudsman+Directions+2026.pdf/7a497eab-60d3-155f-e5cd-357b80ebda24)). On 1 July 2026, RB-IOS 2026 replaced the 2021 scheme ([RB-IOS 2026 text](https://av.sc.com/in/content/docs/in-integrated-ombudsman-scheme.pdf)). On 11 February 2026, RBI also withdrew the January 2021 "Strengthening of grievance redress mechanism in banks" circular (RBI/2025-26/207). It cited duplication with the Financial Statements disclosure Master Direction, RB-IOS 2026 and the IO Directions ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/rbi-withdraws-circular-on-bank-grievance-redress-mechanism); [JHS](https://jhsassociates.in/resources/rbi-withdraws-2021-grievance-redress-circular-for-banks/)). Any engine whose rules cite "MD KYC 2016", the 2017 limited-liability circular or the 2021 grievance circular is therefore citing repealed text. The substance mostly survived; the instrument names did not.

Five clocks dominate a complaint's life. The first is the bank's **30-day reply window**. RB-IOS cl. 10(1)(f) lets the customer escalate if the bank has not replied within 30 days, or within an RBI/NPCI/card-network TAT if that is longer. IO cl. 14(9) separately requires the final decision to reach the complainant **within 30 days of the bank's receipt**. The second is the **IO escalation clock**. Every partially resolved or wholly rejected complaint must be auto-escalated **within 20 days of receipt**. Where an external TAT applies, it must be escalated early enough to give the IO at least 10 days before that TAT (IO cl. 14(1)). The third is the customer's **90-day RBI filing window** under cl. 10(1)(g). It replaces the 2021 scheme's one-year window and runs from the later of the 30-day expiry and the bank's last communication. The fourth is the **Ombudsman-stage clocks**: 15 days for the bank's written response (cl. 14(2)) and 30 days for award compliance or appeal after the complainant's acceptance letter (cl. 15(5), 17). The fifth is the **money clocks**. These are the T+1/T+5 failed-transaction reversal deadlines with ₹100 per day of delay paid without a claim ([RBI/2019-20/67](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/English/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=3074)), and the 10-working-day shadow credit and 90-day liability decision for unauthorised electronic transactions (RBC-CB paras 72–73).

Three structural facts constrain any design. First, **RBC-CB contains no generic acknowledgement or resolution TAT for all complaints**. The only explicit acknowledgement duty is the immediate, numbered auto-response for unauthorised-transaction reports (para 66), and the generic 30-day clock comes from RB-IOS and the IO Directions. Second, **the scheme version is keyed to when RBI received the complaint, not when the bank did** (cl. 20(2)). A complaint that reached the bank in 2025 but was filed with RBI after 30 June 2026 falls under the 2026 scheme's 90-day window and may already be time-barred, even though the 2021 one-year window would have allowed it. That is an inference from cl. 20(2), not an RBI statement. Third, the instruments say "days" without qualification almost everywhere except the limited-liability framework. That framework defines working days by the home branch's schedule and excludes the day the communication was received (RBC-CB para 71). The engine must therefore carry the day basis as an explicit, flagged attribute of every rule, not an implicit assumption.

### Formulas an engine can execute today

Read together, the tables reduce to a small set of executable formulas. **RB-IOS eligibility** is `bank_deadline = max(bank_receipt + 30d, bank_receipt + external_TAT)`, `escalation_open = no_reply_by(bank_deadline) OR customer_dissatisfied`, and `filing_deadline = max(bank_deadline, last_bank_communication) + 90d`. **Scheme selection** is `scheme = 2026 if rbi_receipt >= 2026-07-01 else 2021`. The IO clocks are `io_due = bank_receipt + 20d` for ordinary complaints and `io_due = external_TAT_deadline − 10d` for network-governed ones, with `final_reply_due = bank_receipt + 30d`. A rejection or partial outcome must carry the A18 elements. **Failed-transaction compensation** is `100 × max(0, R − (T + n))` in calendar days, credited without a claim. **Limited liability** is `delay_wd = working_days(home_branch_calendar, alert_received + 1 … report_date)`, with liability 0 if bank fault or `delay_wd ≤ 3`, `min(txn, cap[account_type])` if 4–7, and board policy beyond. The shadow credit is due at `notification + 10 WD` and determination at `receipt + 90d`. The service-event formulas follow the same shape: `₹5,000 × days` (property documents beyond 30 or 60, deceased lockers), `amount × (BankRate + 4%) × delay/365` (deceased deposits), `₹500 × days beyond 7 WD` (card closure) and `₹100 × days beyond 30` (credit information). These are inferences from the instruments' text, with the day-basis caveats above.

Several parameters must remain **configurable and flagged rather than hard-coded**. They include the final RB-IOS 2026 circular number (not found), the NBFC/SFB/PB/PPI/CIC versions of the IO Directions (an often-quoted 25-day CIC escalation figure is unverified), the 1 January 2027 fraud regime's numbers, NPCI stage TATs, the illegible TAT rows, and DPDP Rule 7's start date. Pause semantics are also open. Whether the 30-day RB-IOS clock stops while a bank waits for customer documents was not researched, so the engine must not invent a pause rule.

## Banks keep missing deterministic clocks, and RBI now says so publicly

The demand-side evidence is large and getting worse. The RBI Ombudsman received **13,34,244 complaints in FY2024-25, up 13.55%**. Ombudsman disposal fell to **93.07%** from 95.10%. Loans and advances made up 29.25% of complaints, and credit-card complaints rose 20.04% to become the second-largest category ([RBI Ombudsman Annual Report 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/rbi-ombudsman-disposal-falls-to-93-in-fy25-when-to-approach-for-complaint-redressal)). Trend comparisons need care. The FY25 report restates FY24 as 11,75,075, which equals the FY24 report's 9,34,355 plus 2,40,720 complaints auto-closed in the CMS, so both years must use the same basis. Banks accounted for 81.53% of complaints at the regional offices. The private-bank share rose to 37.53% and small-finance-bank complaints jumped 42%. Foreign banks (9.44 complaints per branch) and payments banks (8.81) far exceeded public-sector banks (1.11). Average turnaround for unauthorised digital-payment complaints at RBI reportedly rose from **36.3 days in FY23 to 68.3 days in FY25** ([Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/ai-enabled-tools-to-streamline-complaint-handling-at-rbi-mos-finance)). The FY2025-26 report had not been published at the time of research.

The composition of the failures matters more than the volume. **37.84% of complaints disposed at the regional offices were non-maintainable**, up from 28.23% two years earlier. The largest single reason (33.01%, or 36,289 complaints) was customers going to RBI without first complaining to the bank. Duplicates made up 18.85%, incomplete information 13.32%, and the bank's commercial judgement 8.53%. A further 2,45,459 complaints were auto-closed by the CMS. On the maintainable side, **51.91% ended in settlement, conciliation or mediation** and only 0.02% (36 complaints) in awards. The usual reading is that banks conceded once RBI was involved, which means many of these complaints were resolvable at the bank or IO stage. Language is a real constraint too. **22.84% of the 9.28 lakh calls to RBI's 14448 helpline were in regional languages** and 70.43% in Hindi, against 6.73% in English ([RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)). An engine that only reads English misses most of the people who complain.

RBI's leadership has named the failure modes with unusual specificity. In March 2025, Governor Sanjay Malhotra called misclassifying complaints as requests or queries "a gross regulatory violation". He said rejections must be examined by a senior functionary empowered to decide in the consumer's interest ([RBI speech](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_SpeechesView.aspx?Id=1498)). He also proposed tracking social-media complaints, treating repeat complaints as systemic, and using AI-based categorisation ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/will-rbi-governors-tough-talk-on-consumer-complaints-lead-to-real-change/76725.html)). In July 2026, Deputy Governor Swaminathan J said "a significant proportion" of complaints the RBI Ombudsman decided for customers had never been referred to the IO. He said closure is not resolution, and that "a hundred complaints relating to the same issue are seldom a hundred independent problems" ([RBI Bulletin](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewBulletin.aspx?Id=24304)). He warned against systems that "inadvertently bypass the IO through classification or process gaps". The Kanungo Committee had found in 2023 that an "overwhelming percentage" of rejected complaints never reached the IO, and that board customer-service committees were largely ineffective ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/will-rbi-governors-tough-talk-on-consumer-complaints-lead-to-real-change/76725.html)). RBI's response was a pendency drive for January–February 2026 on complaints older than a month ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/rbi-launches-two-month-campaign-to-clear-all-pending-customer-complaints-125120500258_1.html)), the mandatory auto-escalation regime, and cost recovery. **₹7.18 crore was recovered from 31 banks** for excess complaint volumes in FY2024-25 ([RBI AR 2024-25](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23441)). Whether the cost-recovery framework survives the February 2026 withdrawal of the 2021 circular is an open inference. Critics such as Moneylife argue that years of warnings about IO bypass have produced no penalties for misclassification. That is a reminder that supervisory rhetoric and enforcement are not the same thing.

The penalties form four clusters. The largest, by a clear margin, is the **limited-liability workflow**: the 10-working-day shadow credit, the 90-day decision and compensation, and the 24x7 reporting channel. It hit SBI, Central Bank twice, Union Bank and G P Parsik. Every element of it is a deterministic clock (B4–B7). The second cluster is **IO non-compliance and generic customer-service directions** (RBL, Muthoot FinCorp, PNB, IPPB), which escalation logic and audit trails address. The third is **KYC** (HDFC, IDFC First), which maps to D2. The fourth is **recovery-agent conduct** (HDFC, Kotak), which maps to C11–C12. A fifth, smaller line, **late fraud and cyber-incident reporting** (SBI 2021, Bharati Sahakari, ICICI), maps to D1 and D3. RBI notes that penalties address regulatory deficiencies and do not split amounts by charge. Penalties also typically follow inspections with reference dates 18–24 months earlier. The design consequence is that a bank needs **evidence it can replay years later**: which rule version applied, what the clock read, when the credit posted. A live dashboard alone does not provide that. No 2025–26 penalty specifically for failing to escalate rejected complaints to the IO was found. Enforcement of the January 2026 regime is therefore still untested.

Operational pain points line up with the speeches. KPMG India's August 2026 analysis lists duplicates, inaccurate classification, TAT monitoring, staff training and the growth of digital-channel complaints as the recurring problems across the receipt, consolidation, classification, routing, resolution and reporting stages ([KPMG India](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2026/08/from-dissent-to-delight-managing-information-asymmetry-in-customer-grievance-process.html)). The Kanungo Committee called for an RE-agnostic common complaint portal, audit of cross-selling to curb mis-selling, a regulatory cost for deficient service, centralised KYC to stop repeated demands, and online settlement of deceased claims. Its recommendation on lost property documents reportedly remains unimplemented ([Moneylife on Kanungo](https://www.moneylife.in/article/kanungo-committee-recommends-penalty-for-deficient-customer-service-audit-of-crossselling-among-others-for-rbi-regulated-entities/71009.html)). Each named failure translates into an engine requirement:

- Misclassification needs a high-recall complaint-versus-query gate whose "not a complaint" outcomes are audited. IO cl. 4(1)(c) defines a complaint as a representation alleging deficiency "with or without seeking relief".
- IO bypass needs state-triggered escalation that the closure workflow cannot skip.
- Closure without resolution needs outcome-quality checks on the final reply.
- Repeat complaints need clustering that names the breached clause.
- Front-door failures (first-resort rejections, duplicates, incompleteness) need a unified register that acknowledges every expression of dissatisfaction and tracks it against the 30-day clock before the customer reaches RBI.

RBI's own systems raise the evidentiary bar further. The CMS has been a portal since June 2019, with Principal Nodal Officer logins and reports. No public bank-side API was found ([RBI press release](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=47383)). RBI plans to embed AI in the CMS in phases, starting with a chatbot and then "advanced processing" for consistency of decisions ([Regulation Asia](https://www.regulationasia.com/articles/rbi-to-roll-out-ai-tools-to-speed-up-complaint-handling)). RBIH has proposed an Automated Grievance Redressal system with intent detection, classification and translation ([RBIH](https://rbihub.in/?p=17770)). DAKSH handles supervisory compliance tracking and cyber-incident reporting ([RBI](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=54503)). MuleHunter.AI was live in 26 banks by 31 March 2026 ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/finance-ministry-asks-banks-to-use-mulehunterai-to-check-digital-fraud)). As RBI's analytics improve, it can reconcile its Ombudsman cases against a bank's own records. One example is complaints decided for the customer that carry no IO-referral flag. A bank whose internal record is inconsistent will be exposed by that reconciliation, not protected by it.

## Existing solutions automate workflow and text, never the obligation itself

### Commercial platforms stop at generic SLAs or at control mapping

Indian banks run grievances on general case-management and CRM platforms configured with bank-specific category and TAT matrices. The one primary confirmation is HDFC Bank's Grievance Redressal Policy. It says complaints are logged in CRMNext, which categorises them, keeps a TAT per sub-category, auto-escalates cases not resolved within TAT, and issues a reference number. Credit-card complaints run separately on Vision Plus ([HDFC Bank policy](https://v1.hdfcbank.com/assets/pdf/grievance_redressal_policy.pdf)). Kotak publishes per-complaint-type TATs in working days and sends customers the exact resolution TAT at logging, which implies a similar matrix ([Kotak](https://www.kotak.com/en/customer-service/grievance-redressal/personal-banking/turnaround-time.html)). Newgen, Kapture and Salesforce market omnichannel case management with SLA routing. Salesforce's Spring '26 deck adds a "Complaint Management Agent" that searches case history, spots systemic issues and drafts "compliant responses", with no accuracy figures (vendor claim) ([Salesforce deck](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/Industry%20-%20Financial%20Services/EXTERNAL-260-Releasely-Slide-Deck-Agentforce-Financial-Services-.pdf)). Pega's complaints flow captures every action in an audit trail ([Pega brief](https://www.pega.com/system/files/resources/pdf/complaints-executive-brief.pdf)). Pega Smart Dispute is the most instructive analogue: it ships **packaged, regulation-specific SLA packs** for US Reg E/Reg Z, Canada's C-86 and the UK's Section 75 ([Pega](https://pega.com/industries/financial-services/smart-dispute)). **No vendor was found marketing an RBI equivalent.** That means no packaged three-state outcome, auto-transfer to the IO, 20-day or TAT-minus-10 windows, A18 reply content, RB-IOS cap checks, or computation of failed-transaction or limited-liability compensation. That is a "none found" claim, not proof of absence. The notes found no usable sources for Nucleus, Intellect, Kiya.ai, Finacle CRM, TCS BaNCS, Dynamics, Freshworks, Zoho or several Indian conversational-AI vendors.

The regulatory-technology half of the market models obligations as text and tasks, not as case-level logic. In India, RBI/2023-24/117 forced banks to adopt workflow-based compliance monitoring. TeamLease RegTrack answers it with owners, periodic tasks and deviation approvals. It claims a legal database of 1,536 Acts and 69,233 compliances (vendor claim) ([RegTrack](https://teamleaseregtech.com/product-services/regtrack-financial-institutions/)). Jocata's One Case Manager consolidates AML, fraud and sanctions alerts, which is financial-crime casework rather than complaints ([CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/press-release/jocata-launches-one-case-manager-enabling-financial-institutions-comply-by-rbis-single-dashboard-circular/)). Globally, CUBE claims to monitor about 10,000 issuing bodies and align rules with controls ([Microsoft UK blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-gb/industry/blog/?p=71106)). Corlytics runs a hybrid AI-plus-human obligation and traceability pipeline and claims 99%+ model accuracy against a ~98% human benchmark, with no independent audit found ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/aml-tech-forum-usa/2025/12/11/can-data-make-sense-of-an-ever-changing-rulebook/)). Ascent extracts each obligation as a versioned data object into a library of 400,000+ obligations ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2025/10/13/how-ascentai-is-powering-the-next-generation-of-regulatory-compliance-automation/)). These tools are good at what changed in the rulebook and which policy covers it. None turns a parsed obligation into an executable rule applied to an individual complaint. When RBI moved from the October 2025 IO draft to the January 2026 final text, someone still had to reconfigure the CRM by hand.

Vendor use of LLMs is consistently **advisory**. LLMs summarise, draft, suggest and extract, while humans or static configuration decide escalation, compensation and closure. Corlytics' rationale for human-in-the-loop is that subtle phrasing changes reshape obligations ([fintech.global](https://fintech.global/2025/11/11/is-explainable-ai-the-missing-link-in-regulatory-compliance/)). A UK motor-finance redress deployment puts it as "machine handles intake, extraction and drafting; human owns the determination" ([ResultSense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-09-04-manx-financial-ai-complaints-redress/)). In this market, "explainability" means showing a reviewer the source text. It does not mean emitting a decision trace of rule, clause, inputs, comparison and outcome.

### Open-source code is either RBI-flavoured and shallow, or rigorous and generic

The code-level review cloned or fetched 21 repositories and read their source ([GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=RBI+compliance&type=repositories)). **Every RBI-specific project has 0–10 stars and is a student, portfolio or hackathon build. None implements RB-IOS timelines, the 30-day window, IO escalation or the failed-transaction TAT.**

- **ComplianceIQ** chunks RBI, SEBI and IRDAI PDFs with a fixed-size character window. It infers the regulator from the filename and stores no circular number or date. It leaves supersession to an LLM prompt asking whether excerpt B "supersede[s], contradict[s], or simply add[s] to A". Its RAGAS evaluation passes citation labels instead of retrieved text as contexts, against a two-item golden set of placeholders ([generator.py](https://github.com/pavith-raj/ComplianceIQ/blob/main/backend/app/services/generator.py); [run_eval.py](https://github.com/pavith-raj/ComplianceIQ/blob/main/backend/app/eval/run_eval.py)).
- **ComplyNext's `npa_engine.py`** is the one deterministic RBI rule engine found. Its docstring correctly argues that DPD thresholds are "fixed legal rules", and its results carry a `rule_reference` and plain-English `reasoning`. But it covers loan asset classification, hard-codes thresholds without effective dates, and tests only through a `__main__` demo ([npa_engine.py](https://github.com/Nevin100/ComplyNext-Minor_Project/blob/main/backend/src/rules_engine/npa_engine.py)). Its scraper's `RBI/\d{4}-\d{2,4}/\d+` regex is a reusable trick for locating circular bodies.
- **rbi-ombudsman-analytics** is the only repo aligned to RBI Ombudsman categories. It trains a TF-IDF plus LinearSVC on entirely synthetic narratives (its author notes RBI publishes none) and reports F1 = 1.0 on 10 examples per class ([metrics.json](https://github.com/Prasanthdj8/rbi-ombudsman-analytics/blob/main/classifier/metrics.json)).
- **The weaker repos:** banking-compliance-rag-chatbot commits a Groq API key and indexes Basel and FATF documents rather than RBI circulars ([rag.py](https://github.com/nithinraj49/banking-compliance-rag-chatbot/blob/main/rag.py)). ReguMind_AI's advertised risk logic is an empty file ([risk.service.js](https://github.com/shivampatra2027/ReguMind_AI/blob/main/backend/src/services/risk.service.js)). Regulatory-Change-Autopilot's gap analyser lets the LLM emit `deadline: str | None` as free text, not a parsed date ([gap_analyser.py](https://github.com/Manas-singh14/Regulatory-Change-Autopilot-AI-Powered-Compliance-Intelligence-System/blob/main/src/rag/gap_analyser.py)).

Most of these repos carry no licence, so only their patterns can be borrowed.

The rigorous projects are generic, and each solves one piece:

- **OpenFisca** (AGPL-3.0; copy the pattern, not the code) keys formulas by start date through `formula_YYYY_MM_DD` methods. It keeps parameters as value histories with per-value `reference` metadata, resolves them with `_get_at_instant`, and traces every computation through `FullTracer` ([variable.py](https://github.com/openfisca/openfisca-core/blob/master/openfisca_core/variables/variable.py); [parameter.py](https://github.com/openfisca/openfisca-core/blob/master/openfisca_core/parameters/parameter.py)).
- **Catala** models legal exceptions directly and forces authors to choose `RoundUp` or `RoundDown` in date-plus-duration arithmetic ([definitions.ml](https://github.com/CatalaLang/catala/blob/master/compiler/shared_ast/definitions.ml)).
- **Blawx** produces natural-language justification trees from s(CASP). ### The methods literature favours a neuro-symbolic split

Research on machine-executable regulation points the same way. BIS Project Ellipse and the FCA/BoE Digital Regulatory Reporting pilots got as far as proof of concept. The independent review of DRR told regulators that a **precise data dictionary** must come before machine-executable rules ([FCA/PA Consulting review](https://fca.org.uk/firms/pa-consulting-digital-regulatory-reporting-review-phase-1-and-2-digital-regulatory-reporting); [BIS Ellipse](https://www.bis.org/about/bisih/topics/suptech_regtech/ellipse.htm)). LLM-generated formal rules need review: an LLM-plus-SMT study found code generation only **86.2%** accurate. The same study scored compliance-restoration F1 at **1.00 for LLM+SMT versus 0.31 for the LLM alone**. The problems were small, about 13 variables, and the pure LLM was biased towards "illegal" findings and cited vague, untraceable provisions ([arXiv 2601.06181](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06181v1)). Even commercial legal RAG tools hallucinated **17–33%** of the time in the Stanford study ([arXiv 2405.20362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362v1)). The UK Financial Ombudsman Service estimated that AI likely contributed to up to 35% of responses to its initial assessments, and warned of disproportionate verification work ([Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/04/fos-responds-to-the-mills-review/)). Regulatory RAG has a specific temporal failure. Flat chunks cannot tell a 2022 circular from the 2024 version that superseded it, so researchers propose knowledge graphs with `AMENDS`/`SUPERSEDES` edges and in-force reconstruction ([ComplianceNLP, arXiv 2604.23585](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23585); [SAT-Graph RAG](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2505.00039v5)). Answer-quality metrics can also be gamed. In the RIRAG-2025 shared task, a system scored 0.947 on RePASs by copying obligation sentences without generating answers, while a genuine system scored 0.639 ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.regnlp-1.1)).

Complaint NLP evidence is thin and humbling. In a CFPB comparison, TF-IDF plus logistic regression reached macro-F1 0.748 and DistilBERT 0.752, against 0.676–0.685 for Claude Haiku 4.5 zero- and few-shot. Part of the LLM's error came from a naive label parser rather than the model ([results_table.md](https://github.com/carmenwu0520/cfpb-complaint-nlp/blob/main/results_table.md)). AWS reports **97.5%** complaint-versus-non-complaint accuracy, but on 50 synthetic transcripts ([AWS for Industries](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/industries/automate-customer-complaint-classification-with-ai-agents-on-aws/)). On code-mixed Hinglish cybercrime complaints across 14 classes, the best model reached only **74.41% accuracy** ([arXiv 2412.16614](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16614v1)). MuRIL beat IndicBERT on Hinglish tasks by 1.36–13.07 points ([arXiv 2506.16066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16066v1)). No public Indian banking complaint dataset exists. On governance, FCA's 2024 review of 40 firms demanded granular MI, evidenced board challenge and post-implementation reviews of root-cause fixes ([Linklaters](https://financialregulation.linklaters.com/post/102jrak/fca-feeds-back-to-firms-on-complaints-and-root-cause-analysis)). SR 11-7-style model risk management and FREE-AI both require inventories, validation and monitoring ([SR 11-7](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm)). Tamper-evident logs can follow the RFC 6962 Merkle pattern ([RFC 6962](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc6962)). The defensible conclusion is that **LLMs should perceive and draft, and dated deterministic rules should decide.** Every decision should leave a trace that survives an inspection two years later.

## CustomerPulse runs on a US data model and a manufactured clock

The code under `backend/app/compliance` was re-read for this report on branch `research/rbi-compliance-engine` (HEAD e9bb274). The findings below cite file and line, and they split into structural, semantic and assurance defects.

**The input mapper manufactures acknowledgement and resolution.** `ProcessingService._compliance_input` sets `resolved_at = complaint.processed_at` whenever AI status is COMPLETED (`backend/app/services/processing_service.py:417`). It also passes `acknowledged_at=complaint.processed_at` (`:431`). The pipeline sets COMPLETED and `processed_at` immediately before evaluation (`:146-148`, `:379`, `:174`). As a result, every cleanly enriched complaint arrives at the engine already "resolved", so RBI-RES-030 (`backend/app/compliance/engine.py:122-128`) and RBI-CUST-UNRESOLVED-001 (`engine.py:177-184`) return early. Complaints routed to human review, which are the risky ones, are the only ones treated as open. The acknowledgement rule compares `processed_at` against `date_received + 3 days` (`engine.py:96-102`), so any historical import processed later fires RBI-ACK-001 regardless of what the bank actually did. The SLA breach flag combines a hard-coded 30 with `complaint.timely_response is False` (`processing_service.py:418-420`). `timely_response` is a CFPB import field. The SLA service's breach-risk test is likewise urgency ≥ 70 combined with `timely_response` not True (`backend/app/sla/repositories/sla_repository.py:189-204`).

**The data model and taxonomy are CFPB's, not RBI's.** The `Complaint` model carries `company`, `company_response` and `timely_response` (`backend/app/models/complaint.py:36-38`). The rule-based classifier maps to CFPB product labels such as `cfpb_product_debt_collection` and `cfpb_issue_payment_transfer` (`backend/app/ai/ml_models/classifier.py:261-273`). None of the facts the RBI clocks need exists as a field. Those include bank receipt date, outcome state, IO referral date, transaction date T, reversal date R, channel type for TAT lookup, account type for liability caps, home branch and state for the working-day calendar, and RBI receipt date for scheme routing. This is the DRR lesson in miniature: no rule can be correct until the data dictionary is.

**Nine of 17 rules cannot fire from the pipeline.** The mapper never sets `amount_disputed`, the KYC fields, `customer_notified_at`, `customer_service_breached`, `missing_documents`, `is_incomplete` or `missing_investigation_evidence`. All of these exist on `ComplaintComplianceInput` (`backend/app/compliance/models.py:96-104`), but the constructor at `processing_service.py:422-458` omits them. It also builds `AISignals` without `fraud_risk_score` (`:433-438`), although `RiskSignalService` computes one (`backend/app/intelligence/risk_signal_service.py:80`). RBI-HV-001, the three KYC rules, RBI-CUST-NOTIFY-001, RBI-CUST-SERVICE-001 and the three DOC rules are therefore unreachable. Severity is derived from urgency alone: `"high" if urgency_score >= 70 else "medium"` (`:434`). RBI-RESP-001 checks that a `resolution` exists, but the mapper fills it with `enrichment.next_action` when no human resolution is present (`:443-447`). An AI suggestion such as "Manual agent review required" therefore satisfies a resolution check.

**The engine flags its own legally required wording as a legal threat.** `_combined_text` includes `response_fields.draft_response` (`engine.py:217-230`). `LEGAL_REGULATORY_KEYWORDS` includes "ombudsman" and "regulator" (`engine.py:19-28`), matched by plain substring (`engine.py:107-109`). IO Directions cl. 14(12) *requires* a partial or rejection reply to advise the RBI Ombudsman route and give cms.rbi.org.in and the CRPC address. So a correctly drafted reply triggers RBI-LEGAL-001, which is high severity with a one-day compliance-officer review. The same substring test means "court" matches "courtesy". The fraud test (`engine.py:206-215`) matches "fraud" in "this is not a fraud claim", uses only the US spelling "unauthorized", and misses Hinglish, OTP or phishing language and "ATM did not dispense cash", which is the commonest failed-transaction complaint in India.

**Deadlines are calendar days from the wrong anchor, and some are born overdue.** Every required-action deadline is `complaint.date_received + timedelta(days=rule.action.deadline_days)` (`engine.py:78`). That uses calendar days, with no calendar service, no IST handling, and no anchor other than intake. RBI-RES-030 fires only after day 30 but sets its action deadline at day 30 (`backend/app/compliance/rules.json:54-66`), so its required action is overdue the moment it exists. RBI-RESP-001's deadline is day 0 (`rules.json:51`). RBI-RES-030's action type is `notify_regulator` (`rules.json:61`). No RBI instrument in the catalogue requires a bank to notify RBI that a complaint is 30 days old. The actual obligations are IO escalation by day 20, a final reply by day 30, and an RB-IOS advisory in the reply. The 3-day acknowledgement (`engine.py:97`), the 7-day notification (`engine.py:173`) and the 15-day "customer protection threshold" (`engine.py:181`) have no clause behind them in the research. The owner enum offers only agent, manager and compliance officer (`models.py:27-31`), with no Internal Ombudsman, senior reviewer, PNO or fraud-operations owner.

**Risk aggregation and justification inflate and mislabel severity.** `aggregate_risk` returns HIGH for any two triggered rules, even two medium ones (`backend/app/compliance/risk_aggregator.py:28-29`). It returns CRITICAL when rules from two "families" fire with one high and two mandatory rules (`:21-23`). Families are `rule_id.split("-")[1]`, so RBI-ACK-001, which fires on most imports, plus RBI-FRAUD-001, which fires on any "fraud" substring, yields CRITICAL. A complaint saying "not a fraud, just a courtesy call request" processed a week after receipt would score CRITICAL on three false signals. `risk_justifier._severity_for_rule` infers severity by searching rule text for "critical", "high", "medium" in that order (`backend/app/compliance/explainability/risk_justifier.py:12-17`). RBI-NEAR-HIGH-003, whose configured severity is medium (`rules.json:97`), is therefore reported as high because its template contains "high-severity".

**Evaluation happens once and cannot drive escalation.** `_store_compliance_evidence` evaluates and then returns without storing if any evidence record already exists for the complaint (`processing_service.py:400-401`). There is no scheduler and no lifecycle trigger, so a complaint's compliance state never changes as days pass, a human approves a reply, or a customer escalates. Auto-escalation runs *before* compliance evaluation (`processing_service.py:169` versus `:174`). It triggers on urgency, churn and the `timely_response`-based SLA test (`backend/app/escalations/service.py:219-236`). Its compliance integration is a comment placeholder (`escalations/service.py:202-204`). There is no IO escalation, three-state outcome, senior-review gate or Ombudsman route anywhere in the code.

**Two disconnected rule catalogues, neither dated.** The engine loads 17 rules from `rules.json` through an `lru_cache` (`backend/app/compliance/rule_registry.py:1-14`). `ComplianceRule` has no effective date, version, source instrument or clause field (`models.py:140-146`). Engine and rule-set versions are the constants "1.0.0" (`models.py:8-9`). Separately, `seed_data.py` seeds database rule records with different IDs, such as RBI-SLA-RESOLUTION-001 (`backend/app/compliance/seed_data.py:93`) and RBI-FRAUD-DELAY-001 (`:165`). Each carries a version, status and effective date, but the effective date is an arbitrary `BASELINE_EFFECTIVE_FROM = 2026-01-01` (`seed_data.py:8`) tied to no instrument. The schema that could hold proper dating exists (`models.py:264-276`), but the engine never reads it.

**Explanations are reconstructed after the fact and cite whatever retrieval returns.** `generate_explanation` rebuilds evidence from the current complaint payload through `map_evidence` (`backend/app/compliance/explainability/service.py:94-99`) instead of using the evidence the engine recorded when the rule fired. Its dependency map lists fields that never exist in the payload, such as `regulatory_keyword` for RBI-LEGAL-001 (`backend/app/compliance/explainability/evidence_mapper.py:4`). As a result the explanation's "confidence", which counts missing fields (`rule_explainer.py:42-48`), is downgraded for reasons unrelated to the decision. Source retrieval runs with `status=None` and `min_similarity=0.0` (`explainability/service.py:142-151`). It tags every returned chunk as supporting every triggered rule (`:169`). It does not pass `effective_on`, even though the knowledge-base search supports an in-force filter (`backend/app/compliance/service.py:659`). The only regulation in storage is the RBI Lead Bank Scheme master circular of 2019 (`storage/regulations/RBI/2019/markdown/10MC02072019_1__2.md`). It does not govern complaints, and its Hindi header extracted as garbled glyphs. Any citation the system produces today is therefore decorative.

**The audit trail can be erased by anyone who can reach the API.** In `backend/app/compliance/router.py`, `POST /evaluate` (line 60), `DELETE /evidence/{record_id}` (line 199), `POST /rules` (242), `PUT /rules/{record_id}` (253) and `POST /reason-codes` (277) carry no `require_roles` dependency. The regulatory-document endpoints are protected for ADMIN and SUPER_ADMIN (lines 290–435). Evidence deletion is a hard `db.delete` (`backend/app/compliance/repository.py:85-90`), with no hash chain or legal hold. Separately, `bedrock_region` defaults to `us-east-1` while `aws_region` defaults to `ap-south-1` (`backend/app/core/config.py:32`, `:39`). Unless a deployment overrides it, complaint narratives containing transaction identifiers would be sent to a US region. That needs a payment-data-localisation analysis under D5 before any bank pilot.

**What is genuinely in place.** The engine validates inputs with Pydantic and stores each result with rule IDs and version stamps. Explanation and explain-with-sources endpoints exist. The regulatory document pipeline uploads, converts, chunks by heading with word overlap (`backend/app/compliance/rag/chunker.py:15-62`), embeds and supports admin review, and its chunks carry `effective_from`/`effective_to`. The processing service has a deterministic fallback when Bedrock is unavailable (`processing_service.py:122-126`, `:459`), and a reporting module and compliance test suite exist. The only test touching the mapper asserts response-field mapping (`backend/tests/compliance/test_processing_compliance_trigger.py:149-158`). Nothing asserts `resolved_at`, `acknowledged_at`, SLA flags or the unpopulated fields. The plumbing is real; the regulatory semantics are not yet there.

## A deterministic core wrapped by LLM perception

The target architecture follows from three findings. Penalties fall on clocks. LLMs are unreliable judges of legality. Inspections replay decisions years later. The design therefore puts every regulatory decision in a deterministic, dated, traced core. LLMs perceive (classify, extract, translate) and draft, and they never decide breach, escalation or compensation.

```
 Channels (branch, email, call transcript, app/chat, social, RBI CMS export, NPCI/ODR feeds)
        │
 [1] Intake gateway ── PII vault (Presidio + bank recognisers) ── India-region LLM endpoints only
        │
 [2] Perception (LLM + baselines, enum-constrained output, confidence → human queue)
        complaint-vs-query · RBI category · language/transliteration · fact extraction (₹ amount, T, UTR, VPA, account type)
        │  facts carry provenance (span, model, prompt version)
 [3] Event store (append-only, bitemporal): BANK_RECEIVED, ACKNOWLEDGED, INFO_REQUESTED, OUTCOME_SET,
        IO_REFERRED, IO_DECIDED, FINAL_REPLY_SENT, SHADOW_CREDITED, REVERSAL_POSTED, RBIO_NOTICE, AWARD_*, ...
        │
 [4] Obligation registry (Git, YAML/JSON, OpenFisca-style dated params, clause + URL + verification grade,
        status lifecycle, tests, CI linter) ◄── [9] Regulatory change pipeline (watch → LLM draft → legal sign-off)
        │
 [5] Temporal core: bank calendar service (state + home branch) · clock engine (start/pause/stop, basis, IST, rounding)
        · external TAT table · scheme router · applicability profile
        │
 [6] Evaluator (runs on every event + scheduler tick): per-condition trace, deadlines, breach forecasts,
        compensation calculators → decision records (hash-chained, WORM-anchored)
        │
 [7] Workflow actuators: IO auto-escalation · three-state closure gate · senior review / no same-unit closure ·
        response QA gate · RBIO case module · fraud-case bridge · incident multi-clock · KYC/recovery guardrails
        │
 [8] Explain & report: clause-cited explanations from stored traces · in-force RAG with entailment check ·
        RCA clusters by breached clause · CSCB/IO/RBI cadence reports · CMS reconciliation
        │
 [10] MRM layer: AI inventory, gold sets by language/channel, drift & override dashboards, red-team log, AI incident register
```

**Every decision leaves a record that can be replayed.** The evaluator writes one decision record per rule evaluation. It holds the complaint ID, obligation ID and version, the clause, the anchor date used to select the version, every input fact with its provenance (human event, system feed or LLM extraction with model and prompt version), the comparison performed, the computed deadline, the calendar version, the result and any compensation with its formula. Each record carries the SHA-256 of the previous record, and a daily root is anchored to WORM storage. In-application chaining or S3 Object Lock is preferable to the retired managed ledger (QLDB end-of-support was flagged in the methods notes as to-verify). Explanations are rendered *from* these records. Regulatory citations come from the obligation's clause anchor first. Semantic retrieval is used only for supplementary context, filtered to texts in force on the anchor date and checked by an entailment step.

**LLM perception is bounded by design.** Classification and extraction use enum- or schema-constrained output to avoid the parser failures seen in the CFPB experiment. They are benchmarked against a TF-IDF baseline and measured separately by language and channel, with the high-recall complaint gate tuned to minimise false negatives. Any "not a complaint" or "no deficiency" outcome is routed to human review, which answers the Governor's senior-functionary demand directly. Fraud labels from AI are confined to "suspected; refer for review", because natural justice under the FRM regime requires a show-cause notice and a reasoned order (D1). All prompts and embeddings run on masked text in an India region. Re-identification happens only inside the bank boundary. The rules-only fallback must produce a compliant "pending manual review" state rather than an empty draft that trips RBI-RESP-001.

## A dated roadmap: stop false assurance, then build the clocks before 1 January 2027

The regulatory calendar sets the pace. The DPDP consent-manager rule lands around 13 November 2026, the expanded fraud-liability regime on **1 January 2027**, and most DPDP obligations, including breach reporting, around **13 May 2027** (with the Rule 7 date conflict noted). Inspection reference dates trail penalties by 18–24 months, so evidence produced from Phase 1 onward is what a 2028 inspection will read.

**Phase 0 consists of concrete code changes.**

- **Router:** add `require_roles` to `POST /evaluate`, `DELETE /evidence/{id}`, `POST`/`PUT /rules` and `POST /reason-codes` (`router.py:60,199,242,253,277`), and replace the hard delete in `repository.py:85-90` with a soft-delete tombstone, since evidence should never be erasable.
- **Mapper:** stop mapping `processed_at` into `resolved_at` and `acknowledged_at` (`processing_service.py:417,431`), and leave both null until a human or system business event exists. Remove the CFPB `timely_response` from the breach computation (`:419`). Pass `fraud_risk_score` from `RiskSignalService` (`:433-438`). Stop substituting `next_action` for `resolution` (`:443-447`).
- **Engine text matching:** remove `draft_response` from `_combined_text` (`engine.py:227`) so mandated reply wording cannot trigger RBI-LEGAL-001. Switch keyword tests to word-boundary, negation-aware matching with Indian spellings ("unauthorised") and failed-transaction phrases.
- **Rule semantics:** retire `notify_regulator` as RBI-RES-030's action in favour of the IO and final-reply obligations. Compute action deadlines from the trigger event, not intake (`engine.py:78`). Mark the 3/7/15-day rules as bank-policy rules without an RBI clause, or delete them.
- **Risk scoring:** read severity from the rule object rather than text search (`risk_justifier.py:12-17`), and stop two medium rules escalating to HIGH and two "families" to CRITICAL without a documented rationale (`risk_aggregator.py:21-29`).
- **Re-evaluation:** allow it by versioning evidence records instead of returning on `existing_count` (`processing_service.py:400-401`), and evaluate compliance before auto-escalation (`:169` vs `:174`).
- **Explanations:** use the evidence stored at trigger time (`explainability/service.py:94-99`), set a similarity floor, pass `effective_on`, and attribute citations per rule (`:142-169`).

Each change ships with a test that asserts dates, flags and reachable rules.

Several Phase 1 choices are cheap and decisive. Store the registry as YAML or JSON in Git with OpenFisca-style dated parameters, and copy the pattern rather than vendoring AGPL code. Build the calendar on MIT `holidays` with a bank overlay for second and fourth Saturdays and state NI Act holidays, implementing para 71's "exclude the day received" rule explicitly. Write every timeline from Tables A–C as a scenario file with expected deadlines and outcomes before writing the evaluator, so the tests encode the regulation and the code follows. Load the actual RB-IOS 2026, IO Directions 2026, RBC-CB and 2019 TAT texts into the corpus with paragraph anchors, and fix the Devanagari extraction. Phase 2's B10 work should encode the 1 January 2027 regime as a future-dated obligation whose parameters are marked unverified and block activation until the final text is read. The draft's ₹50,000 / ₹25,000-or-85% / 30-day figures must not be hard-coded.

## Conclusion

The research inverts a common assumption about AI and compliance. The scarce capability is not language understanding but date arithmetic with provenance: knowing which clause version governed a complaint, which calendar the clock ran on, when it started and stopped, and what money was owed. Regulators are pushing banks toward this from both directions. RBI mandates fully automated escalation and three-state outcomes, its own analytics will soon reconcile Ombudsman outcomes against bank records, and its leadership asks for AI that supports judgment rather than replacing it. The engine that wins inspections will be the one whose LLM is the least trusted component.

For CustomerPulse the uncomfortable finding is also the cheap one. Its current compliance scores cannot be presented as regulatory assurance, because the mapper fabricates resolution, the data model is American, and the engine penalises the wording RBI requires. Yet the 25 capabilities nobody offers are mostly small, deterministic pieces: a scheme router, an external-TAT table, a bank calendar, an IO escalation clock, compensation calculators and a reply checklist. They can be built on permissively licensed parts within a quarter, and they line up with the clocks RBI actually fines banks for missing. A bank buying such an engine in early 2027 is buying protection against the next SBI-style penalty, not a chatbot. Several items stay open and should remain flagged in code rather than resolved by assumption: the RB-IOS 2026 circular number, the NBFC/CIC IO variants, the illegible TAT rows, the 2027 fraud parameters, NPCI stage TATs, clock pause semantics, the financial-statements disclosure table and DPDP Rule 7's start date.
